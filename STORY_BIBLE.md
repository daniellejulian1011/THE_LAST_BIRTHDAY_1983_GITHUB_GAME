# THE LAST BIRTHDAY
## Game Story Bible · Psychological / Supernatural 3D Horror · 1983

**Genre:** First-person, story-driven psychological survival horror. Exploration, found recordings, environment puzzles, unreliable voices, player choice, hiding/chase, branching endings.

**Logline:** Ten years after an abandoned children's party palace was sealed, a 24-year-old woman returns to find the brother who disappeared at his seventh birthday party. Every recording insists he is still there. Every voice that calls her name is lying — except for the one that cannot speak.

**Tagline:** **TEN YEARS EMPTY. THE MUSIC NEVER STOPPED.**

### 1. Time, geography, tone

- **Date:** Thursday, **October 27, 1983**. The precise tenth anniversary of the disappearance.
- **Place:** **Candlewick's Celebration Palace**, a family birthday venue on the outskirts of **Hollowbrook, Maine**. It opened in 1968 and was locked after the **October 27, 1973** incident. Boarded windows, water damage, broken neon, leaking ceiling tiles, mold-sweet confetti, 1970s patterned wallpaper, and birthday decorations preserved like evidence.
- **Era:** A hand-held cassette deck, analogue security monitors, rotary telephones, paper attendance ledgers, and electromagnetic hums — no modern technology, no mobile phones inside the story. A pay phone and car radio appear only in ending epilogues.
- **Atmosphere:** The player feels watched and counted, not merely chased. A birthday melody leaks from dead speakers. A booth opens with no electricity. The most alarming moments happen in silence.
- **Visual identity:** Faded coral-red, hospital teal, nicotine cream, tarnished brass, off-black. Deep interior fog, flashlight cones, paper textures, crawling dust, flaking painted mascots, film grain, 4:3 security monitor overlays, hard shadows.
- **Rules of fear:** The story avoids relying exclusively on loud jumpscares. Dread comes from recognizable human objects behaving slightly wrong: the eleventh chair, eleven candles, a portrait with an adult June visible in a 1973 photograph.

### 2. Principal cast

**JUNE MERCER · Player protagonist · 24**  
Born 1959. Protective older sister to Robin. Left Hollowbrook after the police concluded the birthday disappearances were an elaborate abduction. Ten years later, she has survived by refusing to discuss that night. She remembers running to fetch help instead of going back inside; the guilt makes her vulnerable to imitations. Her voice is soft but practical. She keeps a journal and sometimes narrates brief internal observations.

**ROBIN MERCER · Missing child · Seven in 1973**  
June's younger brother. Non-speaking, resourceful, observant; communicated with June through **three careful knocks**. The voice calling itself Robin on the tapes is a trap. Whether a fragment of Robin still exists behind the sound is the central uncertainty. His knock pattern is the only signal the Host cannot imitate because it requires refusing to answer.

**MR. MORROW / THE HOST · Antagonist · Apparent age unknown**  
A towering cracked party mascot with a paper crown, old-fashioned coat, and frozen smile. Children were told he was a performer; no employee records list him. He was given form by an experiment in which a roomful of children were told to answer their names into a microphone until every seat was filled. He imitates loved ones and feeds on recognition — each spoken answer helps him locate and keep someone. His footsteps come from the ceiling even when he is standing behind the player. He is exceptionally quick once June speaks to a tape.

**EDWIN CANDLEWICK · Venue owner · 49 in 1973**  
A charismatic local entrepreneur who transformed a bakery warehouse into a lavish children's birthday destination. As business dwindled, he allowed Dr. Lenore Kells to study children's responses to musical cues in the unused recording studio behind the stage. His last ledger entry is a single line: *“I was certain there were ten.”* He vanished before the fire inspectors arrived.

**DR. LENORE KELLS · Researcher · 38 in 1973**  
Specialist in auditory memory and suggestion. Her research project, **THE ELEVENTH VOICE**, secretly used children's voices, microphones, and looping birthday songs to test whether a repeated chorus could make a group "remember" a person who had never been present. Her warnings on Cassette 02 suggest she realized the experiment was not simply psychological.

**IVY BELL · Survivor / witness · Eight in 1973**  
The only invited child known to have reached the road before dawn. She tied a **red ribbon** around the ledger's brass key and marked clues to the three-knock pattern. The papers reported that she never spoke after the incident. Her later whereabouts are unknown; small drawings of a girl in a red bow appear in the arcade.

### 3. History — what really happened

**1968:** Edwin Candlewick opens the Celebration Palace. Children love the singalong stage, prize machines, balloon arch, and the party mascot Mister Morrow.

**1972:** Kells rents backstage audio rooms under the claim of developing a children's attention program. Eleven microphones are installed; only ten are shown on the wiring diagram. Children are taught the phrase **“I am here.”**

**October 27, 1973 — The final party:** Robin turns seven. Eleven children arrive, and the guest ledger still holds eleven names. During the last chorus, children hear a strange extra voice answer to nobody. After Robin vanishes, the eleventh name on the ledger appears overwritten with “THE HOST.” The speaker system continues after the power is disconnected. The master photograph shows eleven place settings but only ten visible child-sized shadows. Robin is missing. Kells records a safety rule: **“Do not answer a voice that knows your name.”**

**1974–1982:** The palace remains condemned. Contractors report muffled singing; workers keep finding fresh confetti behind sealed doors. Officially, the original disappearances remain unsolved.

**October 27, 1983:** June receives a cassette labeled **“ONE MORE CANDLE”**, apparently recorded that morning. On it a boy speaks in Robin's voice, despite the fact that Robin did not speak. She returns at 11:11 p.m.

**Truth:** The Host is not a ghost of a human entertainer. It is a response pattern turned into a hungry, attentive presence. It can only keep someone who gives it an answer. The children may be fragments trapped in the recordings. Robin's three-knock signal persists beneath them.

### 5. Narrative structure / branches

**PROLOGUE — The Invitation.** June reaches the closed palace in rain. The opening decision is to play the cassette or remain silent. Speaking into a later cassette increases enemy awareness and pursuit speed after the generator starts.

**CHAPTER I — Eleven Seats.** Explore the lobby, read the impossible letter, follow the music to Party Room. Cassette 01 establishes the paradox: an adult June's voice can be heard in an old recording. Choose whether to speak Robin's name or rewind in silence.

**CHAPTER II — The Rule.** The arcade recording reveals Kells's warning and establishes that Robin's three knocks are authentic. June may hide in a prize locker when threatened.

**CHAPTER III — The Ledger.** The Manager's Office provides the brass backstage key, names Ivy Bell, and reveals a counting trick. Each clue makes the true solution more intelligible.

**CHAPTER IV — Light Has a Price.** Locate the fuse, return to the generator, and decide whether to restore power. Doing so awakens the Host and turns exploration into pursuit.

**CHAPTER V — Last Song.** Open the backstage gate, cross the stage, and make a final choice: signal in knocks, sing a name, destroy the transmitter, follow the false Robin, or retreat. Players can alternatively exit the lobby. Three captures by the Host trigger a sixth, failure outcome.

### 6. Endings / consequences

| Ending | Trigger | Outcome | Emotional character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **01 / THE LAST SILENCE** | Use the three-knock counter-signal at stage | Robin’s remaining signal is freed; the Host dissolves | Hopeful, uncanny |
| **02 / THE NEW HOST** | Answer the birthday song with June’s own name | The Host leaves; June replaces it | Identity horror |
| **03 / ASHES & CONFETTI** | Sever the broadcast cables | June escapes; a residual signal may follow | Bittersweet, ambiguous |
| **04 / THE ELEVENTH GUEST** | Follow Robin’s speaking imitation behind the curtain | June becomes another missing guest | Tragic, deceptive |
| **05 / THE GUEST WHO LEFT** | Escape through the main entrance | June survives without knowing what happened | Survival, unresolved |
| **06 / NEVER FOUND** | Get caught by Mr. Morrow three times | June is absorbed into an impossible 1973 photograph | Defeat, time-loop |

### 7. Sound direction

The party tune must be barely recognizable. A warped four-note motif replaces the conventional birthday song. Low synth drones, fluttering tape, machinery thumps, isolated three-knock signals, the low electronic dying cry of arcade machines, stereo whispers and a brief burst of feedback when the player is caught. Current prototype synthesizes minimal music and stingers using Web Audio; production could add commissioned recordings without changing the underlying story.

### 8. Visual and interactive principles

- First-person viewpoint: no third-person switches, no overlaid map markers in the world (only subtle halos around interactable evidence).
- Players should get a clue that prepares them for every major revelation. Robin's three knocks appear long before the final act.
- Each major room has at least one narrative interaction; three evidence records are mandatory for the backstage route.
- Hiding is temporary, not invisibility forever. The antagonist is threatening without a forced instant fail.
- Endings must come from an intelligible choice or failure, not a random die roll.
- Accessibility: text subtitles, keyboard support, high contrast labels, brightness aided by torch, touch controls; a future full release should add remapping, skip-chase setting, and accessibility options.

**Closing line:** *The count was wrong. And it had always known your name.*
